# Catalog and Gap Audit

**Status: portfolio research for proposed advisory programs. No proposed program is approved as a live Zion offering by this document.**

Snapshot: **15 November 2026**. Evidence references resolve through the [source register](source-register.md).

## Scope and coverage

The review inspected the live homepage; service, premium-service, AI-service, solutions, Apps, and Apps Network hubs; selected adjacent product destinations; and public source-repository documentation. It screened all 1,614 records in the historical service index and all 421 unique IDs in the linked five-part flagship app registry. The source repository's top-level tree was inspected without truncation; this was not a recursive audit of every source file or every app repository.

The review did not exercise app features, verify delivery capacity, validate claims of production readiness, or certify every outbound link. Catalog records, public repositories, city sites, app cards, translated pages, and active commercial offers are different counting units.

## Canonical inventory specification: documentation only

The proposed manual inventory should capture canonical offer ID; preferred name; known aliases; category; buyer/job; live page; source-repository listing; evidence date; availability status; delivery owner; proposed related program; review due date; and unresolved issues.

Do not count a translated URL, alias, placeholder, repository, city page, and active service as interchangeable units. Do not merge records solely because names resemble each other. Owners must determine whether similarly named chatbot, warranty, or handoff listings are distinct products, aliases, or stale records.

## Priority and validation

1. **Agent authority first:** strong adjacency to the promoted agent ecosystem, a clear owner/approval problem, and a pilot that can remain entirely documentary.
2. **Supplier exit second:** validate around a real renewal, material supplier change, or buyer continuity concern; do not sell another score.
3. **Revocation and retirement third:** require qualified privacy and model-risk participation because technical feasibility and legal basis can be case-specific.

Before any program launch, record a named sponsor and delivery owner; nearest existing offer and reason this is not duplication; one accepted pilot boundary; evidence sources and limitations; excluded technical work; pricing/capacity approval; legal review where applicable; and an explicit go, revise, merge into an existing service, or stop decision.

If a Zion owner confirms that a current service already provides the same end-to-end deliverables, do not launch a competing label. Merge the proposal into that service's documentation and adjust the cross-promotion plan instead.
