# Claims, Localization & Approval Policy

**Status: documentation and editorial controls for proposed advisory programs. This policy does not establish that any proposed program is available, compliant, certified, or technically implemented.**

Edition: **15 November 2026**.

## 1. Separate the approval layers

The repository owner has approved public publication of the 12 planning documents in this repository. Keep that approval distinct from:

1. Acceptance by Zion of a new program name, differentiated scope, and accountable delivery owner.
2. Validation of staffing, capacity, commercial terms, pricing, and bookability.
3. Legal/privacy/security review of relevant claims and pilot boundaries.
4. Channel- and locale-specific approval of promotional copy.
5. Authorization for any change to a production website, source repository, directory, sibling app, email, social account, or external announcement.

No layer implies the next. Proposed documentation must retain its status label until an explicit, recorded decision changes it.

## 2. Claim record: minimum fields

For each material public claim, record claim ID, exact wording, intended audience/channel, source ID and URL, date reviewed, evidence type, source version where available, relevant scope, limitation, reviewer role, approval date, review due date, and disposition. Sources live in the [source register](../research/source-register.md).

Use these dispositions: draft; evidence needed; fact-checked within scope; approved for a named channel/locale; held; withdrawn. A source's marketing statement is not automatically independently substantiated fact.

## 3. Claim-specific rules

| Claim type | Required treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Program availability | Say proposed advisory program until delivery ownership, capacity, scope, terms, and launch approval are recorded. A published brief is not a live offer. |
| Technical functionality | Distinguish a listed feature, an observed page, a demonstrated operation, and an independently verified capability. This project observed pages and listings; it did not exercise systems. |
| Portfolio totals | State counting unit, canonical ID rules, scope, date, and owner. Do not combine historical service records, app IDs, repositories, translated pages, or city sites. Avoid a homepage total until reconciled. |
| ROI, savings, performance, accuracy, uptime | Require a documented baseline, method, sample/scope, observation period, and approved qualification. Do not invent numerical benefits or import unrelated supplier claims. |
| Security/compliance/certification | No blanket compliant, certified, safe, audit-ready, or regulation-ready promise. Map any narrow statement to qualified reviewer-approved evidence and applicability. |
| Supplier portability | Do not promise export of provider-owned models, protected IP, or trade secrets; do not equate a common API with behavioral equivalence. |
| Continuity/migration | A tabletop does not demonstrate a successful migration, zero downtime, actual failover, or a guaranteed recovery time. |
| Erasure/unlearning | Do not equate a deletion request, source-record erasure, model retirement, output suppression, and machine unlearning. Record coverage, evidence source, and uncertainty. |
| HTTP status | A point-in-time HTTPS HTTP 200 observation concerns the checked URL and time. It does not validate linked apps, offer readiness, every downstream link, or continuous availability. |
| Price or free deliverables | Proposed-program prices remain TBD. Do not imply specialist pilots are included in the existing Free Discovery scope. |

The assurance wording in program names describes an evidence-oriented advisory review, not an independent assurance opinion or legal/security certification.

## 4. Example editorial decisions

| Avoid | Safer, evidence-qualified alternative |
| --- | --- |
| Three new Zion services are now available. | Three proposed advisory programs are documented for review; delivery and launch approval remain outstanding. |
| Our review prevents agent misuse. | The proposed review documents authority, approval boundaries, and unresolved control gaps; enforcement is separately implemented and evidenced. |
| Exit any AI provider with zero downtime. | A proposed one-supplier tabletop identifies export-rights questions, continuity decisions, and blockers; it does not execute migration. |
| Permanently erase personal data from every AI model. | Map relevant artifacts and document case-specific restriction, deletion, retraining, or retirement decisions and known limitations. |
| Hundreds of production-ready apps, in five approved languages. | Explore the current directory; app availability and locale coverage require separate verification. |
| All research links are verified working. | The completion check covers the 12 named GitHub document pages and Zion's homepage at a stated time; other source links retain their recorded snapshot status. |

## 5. Localization workflow

English is the initial editorial master, not an approved campaign. Brazilian Portuguese is the first proposed adaptation because existing commercial pages were observed in Portuguese. Spanish, French, and German are later candidates, not approved editions in this publication.

For each locale, assign a qualified reviewer and record source revision, intended market, terminology, legal/commercial applicability, destination URLs, approved status labels, review date, and final decision. Preserve proposed status, limitations, exclusions, and Discovery-scope caveats. Do not strengthen a qualified English statement during translation.

Do not translate unapproved prices, imply local service capacity or legal coverage, or claim five-language availability merely because other ecosystem pages advertise language editions. Re-review a locale when the master claim or destination changes. Machine translation alone is not final approval.

## 6. Public-data and rights boundary

This public repository may contain original planning summaries, public-source citations, blank templates, and clearly labeled synthetic examples. It must not contain client personal data, actual rights requests, credentials, access tokens, operational logs, supplier contracts, confidential evidence, or identifiable interview notes.

Store sensitive pilot evidence only in separately approved private systems with appropriate access and retention. Do not reproduce substantial third-party materials or imply a license over Zion's software or brand. Cite source owners and distinguish their statements from this project's proposals.

## 7. Publication checklist

- Confirm the intended action is covered by an explicit approval and that the approving party controls the destination.
- Confirm program status, differentiation, delivery ownership, commercial scope, and specialist review as applicable.
- Review every material claim, qualification, and source date.
- Check exact HTTPS destinations and inspect unready/soft-error content even when status is 200.
- Confirm descriptive labels, accessible wording, locale approval, privacy, and attribution.
- Ensure the change remains Markdown-only and does not introduce code, workflows, tests, deployment assets, or tracking technology.
- Record release scope, owner, evidence date, revision, and rollback/removal responsibility.
- If any gate fails, hold the placement and report the issue; do not silently rewrite a claim or destination to imply readiness.

## 8. Corrections

Correct material factual errors promptly within the approved documentation scope. Record the affected wording and evidence in the [changelog](../CHANGELOG.md). Removing or editing an external promotion, changing a production page, or executing any technical action requires the relevant owner's separate authorization; it is not a workaround available to this documentation project.
